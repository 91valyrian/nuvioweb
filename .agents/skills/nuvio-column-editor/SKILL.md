# Nuvio column editor

Work in the nuvioweb repository. Read `references/editorial-style.md` before writing or substantially revising a column. Inspect the current renderer (`src/app/columns/[slug]/page.js`), `.column-article` CSS, two nearby MDX posts and their images; repository implementations take precedence over a stale reference.

## Select the topic

Every column must directly concern website creation: planning, design, content architecture, development, launch, renewal, or operation of a business website. Define the concrete website decision or task the reader can act on before choosing a title. SEO, GEO, AI, branding, photography and marketing are supporting subjects only when the article explains their application to creating or improving that website; do not write standalone general-interest guides about them. A passing mention of websites, a production keyword in the title, or a consultation link at the end does not satisfy this scope. Keep examples, images and the main body centered on the website task. If a proposed subject falls outside this scope, select a relevant website-production angle instead.

Inventory `content/columns/*.mdx` and `src/data/services.js`. Define one primary reader question and its relevant service. If an existing article answers the same question, improve that article and preserve its URL instead of adding a near duplicate. When Search Console data is available, use page/query impressions to prioritize. Without data, call the topic a reasoned opportunity, not a proven high-volume keyword.

Write a useful answer early, then provide specific decisions, steps, a comparison or a checklist. Explain scope, cost factors, risks and preparation when relevant. Link naturally to useful related columns, the appropriate real `/service/...` route, a relevant genuine `/work/...` case if one exists, and a contextual consultation action. Never imply the company has hospital or other industry work absent from `content/work`.

Verify current technical guidance against primary sources; put source links beside the corresponding claims. Distinguish illustrative examples from actual results. Do not invent traffic, success rates, testimonials, rankings, prices, certifications or client outcomes. Do not present dwell time, GA bounce rate, meta keywords or a sitemap as a direct ranking guarantee. Keep the article readable rather than repeating sales slogans.

## Create article and image assets together

Use the documented MDX frontmatter and Figure/Img/Figcaption format in the reference. Every new article must contain at least three distinct body images, plus its own thumbnail. Keep the complete set within eight images: one thumbnail and three to seven body images. Choose the count for the article's useful sections; a short article still needs three body images. Repeated placements, discarded variants, recommendation thumbnails and unused files do not count. For substantial revisions, bring the revised article up to this contract while preserving its URL and publication date. Use the available imagegen skill and built-in image generation for raster artwork; do not silently use a paid API or replace requested artwork with placeholders. Inspect the three most recent thumbnails and at least one body image. Preserve the editorial quality and legibility, while using the recent images to identify repeated palettes, subjects and layouts rather than copying them.

Persist selected images under `public/images/columns/<slug>/` in WebP with accurate 16:9 dimensions. Inspect every image, Korean spelling and crop. A typical thumbnail is 1600×900 with a readable Korean headline and discreet nuvio identity. Use the approved headline-size reference in [editorial-style.md](references/editorial-style.md#approved-thumbnail-headline-scale): `public/images/columns/multilingual-website-planning/thumbnail-v3.webp`. Keep its apparent headline size and line spacing, and compare candidates side by side at the actual list-card size. Judge glyph height rather than total line width, since title lengths differ; avoid both oversized short words and over-shrinking a short headline. Vary art direction without turning the headline into oversized poster typography. Choose palette, medium and composition freely to suit the topic: photography, paper collage, flat illustration, typography-led artwork or 3D can all work. Do not default every article to navy/indigo gradients, white headlines and glossy browser objects. If the latest posts look alike, deliberately explore a different palette and visual treatment; keep contrast and small-card crop readability. Topic pills and 3D objects are optional, not a template.

Before generating, map each body image to its section, meaning, subject and treatment. Use people for human decisions or interactions, objects for concrete materials or details, and environments for place, context or use conditions when those subjects fit the article. A person–object–environment sequence is a useful default, not a reason to insert an unrelated person or room. Vary framing, scale and explanatory purpose; do not fill the post with interchangeable diagrams, browser frames, robots, desk props or near-identical scenes. Photographs, illustrations and conceptual compositions may be mixed when they aid understanding. Distribute images beside relevant sections rather than collecting them at the end. Every body image needs descriptive alt text and a caption explaining its connection to the discussion. Identify generated people, products, offices and other scenes as illustrative; never imply they depict nuvio staff, real customers, actual client work or measured results. Asset names should describe the content, not list keywords.

If generation is unavailable, complete independent writing work but report missing images clearly. Do not claim the post is publication-ready until the referenced images exist.

## Verify and deliver

Before delivery, count the distinct body images actually embedded in MDX (three to seven), confirm the separate thumbnail, and verify all selected assets exist: four to eight images total. Record the image-to-section plan and count in the article evidence. Run `npm run check:content` and `npm run build`. Preview the real article and list with a browser at desktop and mobile widths when available. Check the image crop, white card, type hierarchy, inline links, captions, tables, CTA, related services and recommendation links. Check title/canonical/Article JSON-LD and that the new slug is in sitemap and RSS. Set `updated` only for a substantive edit, preserve `date`, and never change an established slug just for SEO.

Report the article path, image folder, primary intent, source links, validation result and deployment status. The user's writing request permits local creation and preview; it does not automatically authorize production deployment or sending messages to third parties. Avoid promising traffic or automatic indexing.
